APM Lab · Undo Window

Everyone deserves one undo.

A dropped data volume is a bad afternoon if you notice in an hour. It's a lost quarter if you notice after the recovery window closes. This prototype picks the default recovery window that rescues almost every honest mistake without paying to keep everything forever.

01 · The decision

How long should a dropped volume stay recoverable?

Unity Catalog can now restore a dropped volume, with its privileges and tags, within 7 days. As the PM, I have to decide whether 7 days is the right default, and what evidence would justify 14 or 30. Every extra day saves more mistakes, but it also costs storage and delays a hard delete that compliance teams expect.

The problem

Volumes hold raw files such as images, PDFs and model checkpoints. Mistaken drops are often found late, for example when a monthly job fails or an auditor asks for a file. Most mistakes surface within hours, but a long tail surfaces weeks later.

The change

Pick a fleet-wide default window, and later a per-catalog override. Dropped data stays restorable for W days and is then permanently purged.

Success metric

Share of accidental drops that are still recoverable when someone notices. Target: 99% or higher.

Guardrails

Monthly storage for retained dropped data≤ $50k
Max time before a hard purge (compliance)≤ 30 days
Unrecoverable accidents per year (fleet)≤ 300
Relative error of the tail estimate≤ 10%

Stakeholders

  • Data engineers & ML teams: the people who drop things
  • Platform admins: pay for the retained storage
  • Security, privacy & legal: need data truly gone on schedule
  • Support: handles every "please restore my volume" ticket
  • Storage infra team: owns soft-delete and purge jobs
02 · Simulation room

The rare, late "oops," estimated two ways

For each accidental drop, the time until someone notices is a mixture: a fast group (exponential, about 6 hours) and a slow group with a heavy tail (lognormal). We need P(noticed after W days), which is a rare event. Plain Monte Carlo wastes almost every sample on the fast group. Importance sampling draws only from the tail and reweights each draw. 04 · The math, from my Monte Carlo course

Importance sampling: aim where the answer lives

The problem with plain Monte Carlo

To estimate P(delay > 30 days) ≈ 0.2%, plain Monte Carlo draws delays and counts how many land past the window. With 2,000 draws you expect about 4 hits, so the estimate swings wildly from seed to seed. Its standard error, √(p(1−p)/n), is about as large as p itself.

The fix: sample the tail, then reweight

Draw from a proposal g that only lives past the window: the window plus an exponential overshoot. Each draw is then up-weighted or down-weighted by how likely the real world f would have been to produce it:

x ~ g = W + Exp(θ) w(x) = f(x) / g(x) P̂(delay > W) = mean(w)

How do I know it's working?

Every importance sample counts, so the standard error collapses. The page shows both running estimates with ±1.96·SE bands (CLT), the variance reduction factor, and the exact answer as a dashed line, so you can see the importance-sampling band hug the truth.

Why a PM should care

The whole decision lives in a 0.2–3% tail. A noisy tail estimate could make 14 days look identical to 30 days. Importance sampling tells you the tail precisely enough to put a dollar figure next to each extra day of retention.

05 · How I'd run it on real data

From toy delays to real restore logs

-- 1. How long after a drop do people try to undo it?
--    (illustrative tables)
WITH drops AS (
  SELECT volume_id, event_time AS dropped_at
  FROM gov_lab.audit.volume_events
  WHERE action = 'DROP'
), undos AS (
  SELECT volume_id, MIN(event_time) AS first_undo
  FROM gov_lab.audit.volume_events
  WHERE action IN ('UNDROP', 'RESTORE_TICKET')
  GROUP BY volume_id
)
SELECT
  COUNT(*)                         AS drops,
  COUNT(u.first_undo)              AS accidents,
  PERCENTILE(timestampdiff(HOUR, dropped_at,
    first_undo), ARRAY(0.5, 0.9, 0.99)) AS delay_h
FROM drops d LEFT JOIN undos u USING (volume_id);
# 2. Tail probability by importance sampling
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats
rng = np.random.default_rng(102)

def f(x, slow=.2, mu_fast=6, med=36, sig=1.3):
    fast = stats.expon(scale=mu_fast).pdf(x)
    tail = stats.lognorm(s=sig, scale=med).pdf(x)
    return (1 - slow) * fast + slow * tail

def p_late(W_hours, n=2000, theta=None):
    theta = theta or max(24, .6 * W_hours)
    x = W_hours + rng.exponential(theta, n)    # g
    w = f(x) / stats.expon(scale=theta).pdf(x - W_hours)
    return w.mean(), w.std(ddof=1) / np.sqrt(n)

for days in (7, 14, 30):
    print(days, p_late(24 * days))
